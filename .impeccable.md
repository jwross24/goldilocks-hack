# Design Context — Goldilocks

## Users

Maya, 47, manual wheelchair user. Shopping for a sofa under $800. Her body needs a 17-19" seat-height transfer range. Her last apartment had a deep-seat sectional she couldn't get out of independently. She's frustrated by years of furniture not designed with her body in mind — but she does NOT want pity. She wants **respect, clarity, confidence, and precision**.

The brand extends beyond Maya to anyone whose body doesn't fit median furniture: back-injury shoppers, the elderly, tall users, plus-size, new parents. The unifying customer truth: *"I want someone to do the math for me."*

## Brand Personality

**Calm · exacting · warm.**

- *Exacting* is the unclaimed lane in furniture — nobody currently owns precision-as-care.
- *Calm* prevents the tool from feeling clinical or medical.
- *Warm* prevents it from becoming a spec-sheet brand.

The exactness IS the respect. Maya has spent years being told things are "ADA-compliant" or "accessible" without numbers. The radical move is showing her the math, confidently.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Editorial × Tech-meets-craft. Specifically: Apartamento meets Linear.**

- Magazine-grade typography (Source Serif 4 display + Manrope humanist sans) sells "considered."
- Linear-grade monospaced numerics (JetBrains Mono, sparingly) sell "precise."
- Generous whitespace as a *feature*, not an oversight.
- **Light mode, warm off-white** — verdicts read crisp through H.264 compression, light editorial differentiates from the sea of dark AI demos in the hackathon lineup, and warm off-white avoids OLED bloom on judges' screens.
- Real photography only in the Loom video (Maya's living context); UI itself is type-driven.

## Judges & their lenses

- **Wayfair** (Laura Bulkeley, PM — Customer Onboarding/Billing/International Supply Chain): cares about real customer value, shippable, quantified outcome.
- **Subconscious** (Jack O'Brien CEO, Hongyin Luo CTO, MIT CSAIL): cares about long-horizon reasoning, structured output, doing things a wrapper can't.
- **Baseten**: cares about meaningful multi-model use, not lip-service. We use them as the second-opinion critic.
- **Corvera** (YC W26 founder + CPO): cares about clear product thesis, crisp execution, real user empathy — appreciates "fit at scale" as a category move.

## Design Principles

1. **Measurement as respect.** Numbers are first-class typographic citizens, not 11pt gray spec-sheet content. The seat-height value can BE the headline.

2. **No hedge, no pity, no theater.** Say TOO LOW. Don't soften the measurement. No "we think this might work" copy. No progress bars, no confetti, no "great job" affirmations. Maya is doing a logistics task, not earning a badge.

3. **Silence as a tool.** The JUST RIGHT reveal earns weight by being preceded by stillness. Other elements drop to 8% opacity; the matched card stays at full strength with editorial display type.

4. **Show the gap, not just the verdict.** A rejected sofa shows the EXACT inches off — "15.74″ · 1.26″ below transfer range" — so the precision is visible, not just claimed.

5. **Editorial register, not dashboard register.** Sentence-case headlines. Real punctuation (curly quotes, em-dashes, prime marks for inches). The interface feels like a thoughtful Sunday magazine, not a SaaS analytics dashboard.

## Anti-References (Things This Must Not Look Like)

**Confirmed by the user:**
- Generic AI dashboard (no dark + glowing accents, no glassmorphism)
- Disability product trope (no clinical blue, no medical iconography, no pity tone)
- Hackathon-default startup landing (no gradient hero, no rotating sponsor logos)
- Wayfair's actual site

**Added from research:**
- Inspirational disability imagery (wheelchairs, sunrises, "overcoming" language)
- Soft pastel "accessibility" palettes (sky blue, mint, lavender)
- Rounded-everything friendliness (pill buttons, 24px radii everywhere)
- Hedge words in copy ("might work," "could be a fit," "you may want")
- Stock photography of "diverse shoppers"
- Progress bars, confetti, "Great job!" affirmations on the match
- Concierge cosplay ("your personal shopper," "we curate for you")
- Inclusivity-as-marketing ("for every body" copy, representation imagery)
- AI-as-the-hero (sparkle icons, "powered by AI" badges, chat-bubble-first UI)
- Therapeutic/medical aesthetic (soft greens, wellness language)
- Trust theater (badge piles, "as seen in" logos, founder-voice scrappiness)
- Streaming text typing one character at a time
- Chat-as-the-product (centered chat column)
- Purple/cyan/neon gradients
- 3D blobs, Spline scenes, particle backgrounds
- Lucide sparkle icons everywhere
- Thin weights below 16px (compress badly in screen-record)
- Soft gradients on verdict cards (H.264 banding)
- Skeleton shimmer loaders
- Low-contrast secondary text (#999 on #FFF)
- Busy backgrounds with grain/noise/blurred photos

## The Brand-Defining Single Move

**Treat measurements like a fashion house treats its logotype.**

Every seat height, every depth, every budget number gets the typographic weight of a magazine headline or a brand mark. `17.5″` rendered with conviction, in a distinctive monospaced figure, given air. Not buried in a 12pt gray spec table.

A customer should be able to glance at any Goldilocks page and feel: *someone here cares about the eighth of an inch I care about.* That's the brand. Everything else flows from defending that move.
